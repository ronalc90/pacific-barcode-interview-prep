01How STAR works

The interviewer asks "Tell me about a time when…". You answer with a real story in four parts. Spend most of the time on what you did, and always finish with a result.

S~15% · 15 sec

Situation. Where, when, the context in 1–2 sentences.

"At Western Union, on the Reinstatement team…"

T~10% · 10 sec

Task. What you were responsible for.

"My job was to…"

A~60% · 60 sec

Action. 3–4 specific steps you took. Say "I", not "we".

"First I… Then I… Finally I…"

R~15% · 15 sec

Result. The outcome with a number, plus what you learned.

"As a result… I learned…"

Do

  • Use one specific, real situation, not "usually I…"
  • Put numbers in the result: days, %, volume, SLA
  • Use action verbs: prioritized, coordinated, verified, escalated, resolved
  • Connect the story to this job in the last sentence
  • Pause 2–3 seconds to think. That's normal.

Avoid

  • Talking about the team without saying what you did
  • Stories without an ending or result
  • Blaming customers, coworkers or your employer
  • Answers longer than 2.5 minutes
  • Inventing numbers. Say "approximately" if you estimate.
  • Sharing confidential customer or compliance details

03What to know about Pacific Barcode

Everything here comes from the job posting and their LinkedIn page. Visit pacificbarcode.com before the interview to add 2–3 product names.

  • What they do: label manufacturer and label printing solutions provider. They sell end-to-end color labeling systems (printers, labels, setup, training, support) and have an on-site print production facility.
  • Their value proposition: helping customers move from pre-printed labels to fast, flexible, compliant color labels printed on demand, with a clear ROI (return on investment).
  • History and size: in business since 2002, operations in Southern California and Costa Rica, 11–50 employees. Small team, so each person has a big impact.
  • Culture (use these words): "customers first", "invest in our people to grow", "expect excellence in every job".
  • How they define success in this role: fast responses, accurate orders, on-time deliveries, happy customers, steady revenue growth.

11Useful English phrases

For when you need time, didn't understand, or want to sound structured.

To buy time

  • "That's a great question. Let me think of a good example."
  • "Give me a second to pick the best example."

When you didn't understand

  • "Could you repeat the question, please?"
  • "Just to make sure I understand, are you asking about…?"

To structure your answer

  • "Let me give you a specific example from Western Union."
  • "The situation was… My responsibility was… What I did was… As a result…"
  • "There were three things I did. First… Second… Finally…"

To connect to the role

  • "I'd apply the same approach here when…"
  • "That's very similar to what this role requires, because…"

When you lack experience

  • "I haven't done that exactly, but the closest experience I have is…"
  • "I haven't used that tool yet, but I learned [system] in [X weeks]."

To check your answer landed

  • "Does that answer your question, or would you like more detail?"

12Questions to ask them

Always ask two or three. Never say "No, I don't have questions". Pick the ones that weren't answered during the interview.

  1. "What does a typical day look like in this role, and how many accounts would I manage?"Shows you're picturing yourself in the job.
  2. "How do you measure success for this position in the first 3 to 6 months?"Shows you care about results.
  3. "Which systems do you use for quoting, order entry and tracking?"Lets you prepare for QuickBooks, Smartsheet or an ERP.
  4. "How does the Costa Rica team work with the California operations and production?"Shows interest in the internal coordination part of the role.
  5. "What training do you provide on the products and label materials?"Shows you want to learn the industry. Fits their "invest in our people" culture.
  6. "What are the most common challenges customers bring to this team?"Helps you understand the real work.
  7. "How is the quarterly bonus measured?"Ask near the end, only if compensation comes up.
  8. "What are the next steps in the process?"Always ask this one last.

13Your closing statement

When they ask "Is there anything else you'd like to add?", don't say no. Use 30 seconds to summarize why you fit.

"Thank you for your time. I'm very interested in this role. I've spent more than 12 years being the point of contact for customers, managing requests from start to finish, coordinating with other teams and making sure every detail is correct. I'm excited about learning the label industry and helping your customers and your revenue grow. I'd love to be part of the team."
